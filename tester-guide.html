Tester Guide

Chip Rack — published by Inside Craps

This page is for Chip Rack testers. It describes a pre-release build (0.3.2) and includes a list of known rough edges, so it is not the documentation for the released app. Prefer a copy you can keep? Download it as a PDF.

What this is

A record-keeping app for casino trips. You log what you bought in for and what you walked away with, and it does the arithmetic — per session, per trip, and over time.

Think of it as replacing the notes app, the spreadsheet, or the back of the envelope most players actually use.

What this isn’t

There is no gambling in this app. No wagering, no real money, no play money, no games. It doesn’t connect to a casino, a sportsbook, or a payment system. It’s a ledger — the gambling already happened somewhere else, and this is where you write down how it went.

Worth stating plainly, because anything in this category invites the assumption.

(The name comes from the chip rail — the grooved track around a craps table where players keep their chips. Your rack is what you brought and what you have left, which is exactly what the app keeps track of.)

Your data never leaves your phone

There’s no account to create, no login, no cloud, no sync, and no analytics. Everything you log is stored in a database on your device, and if you delete the app, it’s gone.

The app makes exactly one kind of outside connection: it checks whether a newer version of itself is available. That request contains only the app’s version, its release channel and whether you’re on Android or iOS — no account, no device ID, and none of what you’ve entered. Nothing you log ever leaves your phone. Full detail at https://inside-craps.github.io/privacy.html.

That’s a deliberate design decision, not a missing feature. The trade-off: nothing is backed up automatically. If you want a copy, use the export in Settings (more below).

The core idea: Trips and Sessions

Two levels, and everything else hangs off them.

A Session is one sitting at one game. You record what you bought in for and what you colored up with — the app works out the profit or loss. You can also note the casino, the game, the date and time, and anything else worth remembering.

A Trip is a container for sessions. It has a starting bankroll, and it tracks your running position across every session inside it.

So: a long weekend in Vegas is one Trip. The four separate times you sat down at a craps table across those days are four Sessions.

Two kinds of Trip

Standard trips are what you’d expect — a named trip with a start and end date. “March Vegas Trip.” You create one, log sessions against it, and it closes out when the dates pass.

Local Play is different and worth understanding. It’s a single, permanent, never-ending bucket for your routine play close to home — not for one specific casino. It has no dates and never closes. The idea is that regular local play isn’t really a “trip”; it’s ongoing, and forcing it into trip-shaped boxes would be annoying and would distort your numbers. So it gets its own bucket, and the reports let you look at local play and real trips separately.

You get one Local Play trip, tied to the home location you pick during setup — and you can log sessions against it at any casino in that area, not just one. If you rotate between three places within reach of home, all three belong here.

The home casino you choose during setup is only a convenience: it pre-fills the casino field when you log a session, so the place you visit most often is already selected. You can change it on any session, and doing so doesn’t reassign the trip or affect anything else.

Where you played

Places are organised in three levels:

Region → Destination → Casino

For example: Las Vegas → Las Vegas Strip → Bellagio, or Mississippi → Biloxi → Beau Rivage.

About 214 casinos across 46 regions come built in, so most of the time you’re just picking from a list. If a casino isn’t there, you can add it — Locations tab, find the destination, add it under that. You can also rename a casino or deactivate one you’ll never use, from the casino’s own screen. Anything you add or edit is yours and won’t be overwritten by app updates.

You can’t add a whole new destination or region yourself yet — only a casino within an existing one. If somewhere you play doesn’t fit anywhere in the list, tell me; that’s a real gap and I’d rather hear it than have you force a session into the wrong place.

The built-in list can grow. As of 0.3.2, an app update can add casinos to your existing install — so if a casino appears that wasn’t there last week, that’s intended, not a glitch. Occasionally one may also be corrected or renamed (a few in the list still carry names the property has since changed). Casinos you added yourself are never touched by this, and neither is one you’ve renamed — an edit you made is treated as yours and left alone permanently.

If a casino you play at is missing, please tell me. That’s a genuinely useful report now that the list can be updated in place, and it was the exact gap that prompted this feature: a session at a Pennsylvania casino that wasn’t in the app.

⚠️ If you add a casino and it later shows up in an update too, you’ll see it twice. The app can’t safely tell that your “Hollywood Grantville” and the official “Hollywood Casino at Penn National Race Course” are the same building — and guessing wrong would be worse, since merging two casinos would move your session history onto the wrong one. So it leaves both alone and lets you decide.

This is worth fixing rather than ignoring, because until you do, your Profit/Loss by Casino report splits that one casino across two rows. Two steps, in this order:

  1. Edit any session logged at your version and pick the official casino instead. (Open the session, change the casino, save.)
  2. Then deactivate your own entry — Locations tab, find it, deactivate. It disappears from the pickers, and nothing you’ve logged is lost.

Do it in that order; deactivating first hides the old casino while a session still points at it, which just makes the edit fiddlier. And it’s still worth telling me the casino was missing — that’s how it gets into the built-in list for everyone else.

One quirk that’s intentional: Las Vegas is the only region split into multiple destinations (Strip, Downtown, South Strip–Henderson). That’s because a Vegas trip realistically moves between all three, and the app lets you log a session at any casino in the region your trip belongs to — not just the specific destination. Everywhere else, region and destination are the same thing.

What you can log per session

17 game types are built in: Craps, Blackjack, Baccarat, Roulette, Slots, Video Poker, Poker, Pai Gow Poker, Sic Bo, Keno, and the house-banked poker games — Three Card, Ultimate Texas Hold’em, Let It Ride, Mississippi Stud, Four Card, Casino Hold’em and Caribbean Stud.

Most have variants (Craps: Traditional, Crapless, Bubble, Stadium — Blackjack: 3:2, 6:5, Single Deck, Double Deck, Spanish 21, Free Bet) and many have side bets you can tick off (Craps: ATS, Fire Bet, Repeater, Sharpshooter, Hot Roller).

Variants are pick-one. Side bets are pick-as-many-as-apply. Both are optional.

Adding your own game types, variants or side bets isn’t available yet. The built-in list is what you get for now. So if a game, a rule variant or a side bet you actually play is missing, that’s one of the more useful things you can report — especially craps side bets, where the built-in list is deliberately short and house offerings vary a lot between properties.

(Unlike casinos, which you can add yourself, this one has no workaround. Log the session against the closest game type and tell me what was missing.)

Reports

This is where the logging pays off. Everything is filterable by time period (All Time, This Year, Last Year) and by whether you want Local Play, Standard Trips, or both.

  • Bankroll Trend — your cumulative position over time, with the highest and lowest points called out.
  • Profit/Loss by Casino — where you actually do well, and where you don’t.
  • Profit/Loss by Game Type — same question, by game and variant.
  • Day of Week — whether Saturdays really are worse than Tuesdays.
  • Time of Day — Early Morning, Mid-Day, Evening, Late Night. You can adjust where those boundaries fall in Settings, since not everyone’s “evening” starts at six.

Every chart drills down. Tap a bar or a row and you get the actual sessions behind that number, so a surprising figure is one tap from being explained.

Tax report

Generates a Win/Loss report for your records, which you can share or save to your device as a CSV or a PDF.

There’s a “Report Info & Limitations” screen attached to it — please read that before judging the output. It explains what the report does and doesn’t claim to be.

It is not tax advice, and neither is this document. It organises numbers you entered; what those numbers mean for your return is between you and someone qualified.

Backup and restore

Settings → Export writes your entire database to a single file, which you can send to yourself, drop in cloud storage, or keep wherever you like. Import reads one back.

Two things to know:

Import replaces, it doesn’t merge. Restoring a backup wipes what’s currently in the app and puts the backup’s contents in place. There’s no combining two datasets.

This is your only backup. Nothing is automatic. If you’re logging anything you’d be annoyed to lose, export occasionally.

Known rough edges — please don’t report these

Genuinely useful to know, so you don’t spend time writing up things already on the list:

Dark mode is only partly built. The Appearance setting currently affects the Trips tab only. Every other screen stays light. That’s unfinished work, not a bug.

Appearance changes don’t apply until you restart the app. Picking Dark won’t visibly do anything — and the button won’t even look selected — until you fully close and reopen. Known, being fixed.

One buy-in and one color-up per session. If you rebought three times, you’ll need to either log separate sessions or net it out yourself. Multiple buy-ins per session is a known future addition.

What’s genuinely useful to report

Anything that crashes, loses data, or shows a wrong number. Those three matter most, in that order.

After that: anything confusing. If you couldn’t work out how to do something, that’s a real finding — the app being obvious is a feature, and “I gave up looking for it” is exactly the report that’s hard to get any other way.

Screenshots help enormously. Android has no built-in way to send them to me, so just text or email them.

Please include the version. Settings tab → About, at the bottom — it shows the version, build number and update ID. Paste that into any report. It matters more than it looks: two people can be running the same version number and different code, and that line is how I tell.

Getting started, in about a minute

  1. Open the app. It walks you through a short setup — pick your home location and casino, and optionally a default game and starting bankroll.
  2. That creates your Local Play trip automatically.
  3. Log a session against it: Trips tab → your Local Play trip → + Log Session.
  4. Or create a real trip: Trips tab → + New Trip, give it dates and a starting bankroll.
  5. Once you’ve got a few sessions in, the Reports tab starts being interesting.

Feel free to enter fake data to poke around — it’s your phone, and you can export first or uninstall and start over whenever you want.
